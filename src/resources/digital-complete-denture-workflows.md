Digitizing part of a complete denture workflow changes how the clinic and laboratory share records. A conventional impression or model can enter a digital design process after it is digitized. Other workflows capture selected records directly in the clinic. Identifying which records are digital helps the lab plan the case.

## Keep the approval stages clear

The case still needs records for the intended borders, jaw relationship, tooth arrangement and appearance. Tell the technician which of those items the clinician has approved and which are to be evaluated at try-in. For a repeat case, say whether the previous appliance can be used as a reference and describe what should change in the new one.

A [randomized crossover trial comparing digital and conventional complete dentures](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35850871/) found more border overextension in the digital group at insertion under the conditions studied. After corrections, the trial reported no significant differences at the follow-up assessments. The study illustrates why the clinical review and feedback steps still matter in a digital workflow.

## Send one set of current records

Label the arch, bite records, shade and mould selection, and the requested try-in stage. If you send both an existing denture scan and a new model, state which record supplies the surfaces to preserve. This helps the lab distinguish a reference from the records that govern the new design.
