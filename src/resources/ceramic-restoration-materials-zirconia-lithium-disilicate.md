“All-ceramic” tells a lab that a restoration has no metal framework. The prescription still needs to describe the restoration, the desired appearance and any constraints the selected ceramic has to meet.

## Where the material differences matter

Dental zirconia is a family of oxide ceramics. Different grades offer different combinations of strength and translucency, and each system has its own limits for restoration design and span. A prescription for a zirconia crown is more useful when it also states whether masking a dark substrate or matching a translucent adjacent tooth is the priority.

Lithium disilicate is a glass ceramic. Some systems list veneers, inlays, onlays and crowns among their indications. The exact design and processing requirements come from the selected product's instructions for use. [Ivoclar's IPS e.max guides](https://www.ivoclar.com/en_li/products/digital-processes/ips-e.max-cad) provide one example of how a manufacturer defines those indications.

## What to include in the prescription

- List the tooth number, restoration type and unit count, including any connected units or pontics.
- Share the target shade and tell the lab if a dark preparation, core or underlying component needs to be masked.
- Include the opposing arch, bite record and any clinician-specified constraints on contour or contacts.
- Flag cases where a close match to an adjacent restoration is the main objective. Include its material and shade if known.
