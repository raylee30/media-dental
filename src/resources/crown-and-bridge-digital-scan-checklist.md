A scan can open correctly in CAD and still leave the technician with a question about where the restoration ends or how the opposing teeth meet. A quick review of the exported files helps the clinic and lab catch those questions before design.

## Check the records together

1. **Preparation and margin.** Inspect the finish line from more than one angle. Flag any segment where tissue overlap, a void or a mesh artifact makes the boundary difficult to read.
2. **Adjacent teeth.** Include enough of the neighboring surfaces for the technician to evaluate where proximal contacts should sit.
3. **Opposing arch and bite.** Verify both arches are present and that the bite record represents the intended relationship. Identify any areas the clinician wants the lab to check.
4. **File and prescription match.** Use one case ID across the files, list the restoration units and identify the current version if a scan has been replaced.

## When a rescan is worth discussing

If a margin looks smooth only because the scanner filled in a missing area, flag it and let the clinic decide whether a new scan is needed. A [laboratory study of finish line location and saliva contamination](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36719010/) found larger discrepancies at subgingival finish lines for both scanners studied. The finding is a reason to pay particular attention to how clearly the finish line is recorded.
