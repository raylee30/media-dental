A shade code gives the technician a starting point. A clear reference tells the technician where the match matters most, such as the cervical area of a single central incisor or the lightness of an adjacent crown.

## Send the shade with its context

Name the shade guide as well as the tab code. If more than one tab is relevant, label which one is the main target and explain what the others illustrate. For a translucent restoration, tell the lab what it will cover, such as a natural preparation, core or abutment, and include a preparation shade when it is available.

## Make the photos comparable

- Include a close-up with the selected tab next to the reference tooth and the tab label visible.
- Use consistent lighting, avoid color filters and include a wider view to show neighboring teeth.
- Note the areas that need special attention, such as cervical color, incisal translucency or surface texture.
- Send the original images through the agreed case channel and include only the information needed for the laboratory work.

An [experimental comparison of dental shade photographs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31840142/) found that polarized photography can provide useful color information, although its measurements did not consistently meet the study's threshold for an acceptable match. The written shade prescription and clinician's assessment still matter.

If the desired result is a match to a specific tooth or restoration, mark it in the case notes so the same reference is used from design through final review.
