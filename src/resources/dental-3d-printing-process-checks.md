A printed dental model may be used for a diagnostic review, to make an appliance or to evaluate a restoration. Decide how it will be used before choosing how to check it. The details that matter for one use may differ from those needed for another.

## Validate the process on representative files

Record the printer, material, print profile, orientation and post-processing steps used for each trial. Compare finished models with the reference file or model. Check the regions that matter for the intended use, such as an incisal edge, prepared tooth or the width of an arch.

A [study comparing print technology, layer height and orientation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38670838/) found that accuracy varied by the combination tested. Its results are specific to that study's equipment and settings. When the printer, material or profile changes, check that the new process still meets the lab's acceptance criteria.

## Keep the model separate from the appliance specification

A model resin and a material intended for an intraoral device serve different purposes. Follow the material manufacturer's instructions for the specific application and apply the necessary checks to the final item. For U.S. medical device manufacturing context, the [FDA's additive manufacturing guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/technical-considerations-additive-manufactured-medical-devices) explains considerations for process characterization and validation.
