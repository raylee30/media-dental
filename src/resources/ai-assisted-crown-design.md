AI-assisted CAD can generate an initial shape for a single crown. That can make the technician's first pass faster. The useful question for a lab is how much work remains between the proposed shape and a design ready for production.

A [2025 bench study of AI-assisted crown design](https://www.jap.or.kr/pdf/10.4047/jap.2025.17.1.1) reported shorter design times for the software and scan data studied. A lab evaluating another system should measure design time on its own case mix.

## Keep the review tied to the prescription

Before the design is released, a technician still needs to review the finish line against the available scan, compare the proximal contacts and occlusal relationships with the case records, and check the proposed contours and material requirements. A proposal can look anatomically plausible and still need an adjustment to fit the prescription.

## Measure the whole workflow

When comparing software, record the time spent generating the first design and the time spent correcting it. Note which cases needed manual margin review, contour edits or clarification from the clinic. Include the technician's review and any rework when judging whether a tool helps.

A readable margin, a useful bite record and a specific prescription give the technician a better basis for review in both workflows.
