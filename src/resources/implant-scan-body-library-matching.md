A scan body makes the restorative position of an implant legible to CAD software. The technician matches its scanned shape to the corresponding digital library entry. The implant connection and the scanned component both matter when making that match.

## What the lab needs to identify

- The implant manufacturer, system and platform or connection, with the restoration site identified.
- The scan body manufacturer and reference number, including whether the scan was taken at implant or abutment level.
- The CAD library name or version when the clinic knows it, and any component or restorative preference already specified.
- The scan containing the scan body, the opposing arch and bite, and any separate tissue or emergence profile scan included with the case.

[Medit's scan body matching documentation](https://support.medit.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033527711--Scan-Body-Library-Matching) shows how library data are aligned with scan data. The documentation is a useful example of why it helps to identify the component as well as provide the mesh.

## When to stop and clarify

If the implant system or scan body is uncertain, send the available component record with the case. Two components can look similar on a screen and require different library entries. A missing reference is easier to resolve before the technician has committed to a design.
